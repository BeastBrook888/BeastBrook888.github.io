Matchup-level defensive analysis · 2017-18 → 2025-26

Who actually gets hunted on the projected Sixers?

The hypothesis was that Embiid and Jaylen Brown are the solid defenders while Maxey and LeBron are liabilities. Nine seasons of NBA matchup data say that's right about three of five — and that the man opponents should attack is LeBron, not Maxey. Three independent methods were run against each other. They first disagreed completely — then pooling five seasons of rebuilt lineups showed most of that disagreement was noise, and all three now agree on who the best defender is.

✓
Premise confirmed 5 September 2026. All five — Maxey, Embiid, LeBron, Jaylen Brown and VJ Edgecombe — appear on Basketball-Reference's 2026-27 Philadelphia roster. Every earlier version of this page carried this as an unverified assumption; it is now checked. The roster also brings Dean Wade, who is 5th in the league by pooled dRAPM, and Anfernee Simons, a sixth high-usage guard who is a defensive negative.

Method

Why the naive version of this is wrong

Centres get charged their man's blended FG% while actually facing rim attempts, so the untreated metric ranked small guards best and every good centre worst — it was measuring position, not defence. With a single blended baseline it correlated −0.73 with the share of threes a defender faces. Splitting 2s from 3s cut that to −0.36; only residualising on top got it to −0.01. The chart below plots those last two stages. Each confound was found by a face-validity check, not assumed.

Season by season

Consistency is the whole argument

Single-season intervals are wide, so no one year proves anything. What does carry weight: Embiid is below the zero line in all nine seasons and LeBron is above it in all nine, across three different teams.

Burden vs quality

Edgecombe was never tested

Assignment burden says how featured the men a defender guarded were — it measures the difficulty of the job, not how well he did it. Brown carried the heaviest load of the five. Edgecombe carried the lightest by a wide margin, which is what makes his average rookie grade the least informative number on this page.

Cross-check

Where the second method disagrees

On/off is reported as a check, not a verdict: it systematically penalises high-minute starters, whose on-court minutes face opposing starters while their bench minutes face benches. Negative means the team defended better with him on the floor.

✓
Agree on Embiid and Brown. Embiid is best by both methods and by a wide margin — negative in all nine matchup seasons and all ten on/off seasons. Neither method makes Brown elite.
≠
Disagree on LeBron and Maxey. Matchup data ranks LeBron worst of the five; on/off ranks him second-best. Maxey is the mirror image. Both left unresolved rather than reconciled.
Lineup exposure

What a hunt target costs

Points per 100 possessions above baseline as an opponent shifts a share of its attacks off the lineup's two best defenders and onto its worst. It prices the reallocation, not the absolute level — and in both scenarios the target is LeBron.

Third method

Rebuilding the lineups changed the answer

On-court five-man units were reconstructed from play-by-play substitutions, then intersected between both teams into segments where both lineups are constant, then fed to a ridge-regularised adjusted plus-minus. Unlike raw on/off, both teams' fives are in the design — which is precisely the starter-vs-bench artifact that made on/off unusable.

✓
The earlier “no consensus” finding was largely single-season noise. Rank agreement went from +0.10 to +0.50 against the matchup metric and from −0.20 to +0.80 against on/off. The one-season dRAPM predicts its own pooled counterpart at only +0.30 — which is the clearest evidence that it was measuring mostly noise, and why it was reported as no verdict rather than as a contradiction at the time.
The offensive question

Does staggering actually work?

The usage study concluded these five cannot fit inside the 100 usage points a lineup has — even at each player's most deferential observed rate they sum to 132.5. That made minute allocation, not ball-sharing, the implied lever. This tests whether that holds historically: among teams that assembled three-plus high-usage players, did the ones who kept them apart do better?

⚠
This is underpowered, and more history barely helps. Doubling the window from 13 seasons to 27 moved the comp count only from 16 to 23. Teams that newly assemble three high-usage players are structurally rare — roughly one a year — so this test is limited by how the NBA works, not by how much data was collected. The overlap estimate also moves with sample size (−0.16 → −0.11 → −0.20), which is what an underpowered estimate looks like. It rules out a large, obvious staggering effect; it cannot settle the question at a realistic effect size.
ℹ
The 2017-18 Rockets remain a real existence proof that staggering two ball-dominant guards can work. This says it is not a general law.
Shot diet

Do they want the same shots?

Usage counts a putback and a 19-second isolation identically, so it cannot answer this. Shot locations can. Each player's attempts are split across 16 court zones and compared pairwise — read against the league distribution of similarity, because “0.95” means nothing on its own.

⚠
Maxey and Edgecombe are the sharpest fit problem in the shot data. At 0.989 they are more alike than 99% of league pairs — the backcourt meant to be the future wants the same shots from the same places. Embiid is the opposite: the least similar to everyone, and the least self-creating. He is the one who fits alongside rather than instead of.
What the lineup concedes

The drop-coverage question, finally tested

Every shot is assigned to the segment it falls in, which names both fives — so 638,659 shots are attributable to the five defending them. Early in this project I predicted Embiid's drop coverage plus weak point-of-attack guards would leak pull-up threes and floaters, and framed that as a risk.

✓
The mechanism is real; my reading of it was wrong. Embiid does push shots off the rim into mid-range and floaters — the drop-coverage signature. But that is the coverage working: those are the two least efficient zones in basketball, three-point rate falls 2.3 points and opponent FG% falls 0.9. The pull-up three I warned about barely moves. The real exposure is the reverse — rim attempts climb to 33.6% when he sits.

Two more methods

On/off done properly, and the playoffs

Two tests the earlier work left open. On/off computed from the rebuilt segments can do something Basketball-Reference cannot — hold a teammate fixed, so the comparison is the same guard with and without Embiid rather than starters against bench. And nine playoff seasons ask whether any of this survives in the games the roster was actually assembled to win.

✓
Embiid gets better in the playoffs, not worse. His shift of −2.41 is the largest favourable move of the five, against a league-wide shift of −0.06. LeBron (+1.93) and Brown (+1.14) both go the other way — and Brown's is on the largest sample of the five, 125 games. This is now the fourth method agreeing that Embiid is the best defender in the group.
⚠
One correction worth stating. The first version of this comparison put the playoff figure (raw) against the regular-season figure (residualised for shot mix) and concluded Embiid degrades in the playoffs. Putting both on a raw basis reversed it. Only the shift is interpretable here — the levels still carry the positional bias, which cancels in the difference.
Limits

What this cannot tell you

  • No tracking data. stats.nba.com is IP-blocked from the machine this ran on, so contests, rim deterrence and time-of-possession are unavailable. "His presence discouraged the shot" is not measurable here — only what happened to the man he was assigned.
  • Assignments are nominal. Scoring attribution is internally exact (verified to 0.0000), but who was responsible is NBA's attribution, imperfect on switches and zone. A help defender who roams off his man wears his man's open looks.
  • Perimeter shooting defence is effectively unmeasured — dFG3% failed its reliability test (r=+0.10) and was dropped, so nothing here rests on it.
  • Embiid's samples are thin and post-surgery — 38 games in 2025-26, 19 in 2023-24.
  • Edgecombe is one rookie season on the softest assignments of the five.
  • The adjusted plus-minus is one season only. That forces heavy shrinkage — Embiid's estimate is 62% prior, 38% his own play — so his near-zero dRAPM means too few possessions, not average defender. Multi-year would fix this and is the obvious next step.
  • 2.15% of team-periods could not be resolved and were dropped, so ~4.7% of scoring falls outside a usable segment.
  • Possessions are estimated, not counted — FGA − OREB + TOV + 0.44·FTA, where 0.44 is a league-average constant.
  • Opponent shot-profile allowed is still not built. The lineups now exist to support it, but that analysis has not been run.
  • The staggering test rests on 16 teams. Thirteen seasons of reconstruction was not enough; the star threshold had to be loosened to 24% usage to reach even that. Treat the null as "no large effect visible", not as "no effect".
  • Pooled RAPM assumes a player's impact is stable across the window. That is least true for LeBron, who ages from 36 to 41 inside it. One coefficient per player is the price of the extra data.
Sources: NBA matchup box scores 2017-18–2025-26 (2,009,953 defender×offense rows, 10,637 games) and Basketball-Reference on/off for 34 team-seasons.
