Article 04 · June 11, 2026 PyTorch LSTM

Mikal Bridges: How Probable Was The Iron Man’s Streak?

Read on the SAGB site →
The question

Bridges has played 638 straight games in an era built around resting people. How improbable is that — and can a model estimate, game by game, the chance a player sits out the next one using nothing but workload?

Data

Pulled through nba_api: full game logs for every player on a Mike Brown or Tom Thibodeau roster across their head-coaching careers, at roughly 11,700 player-game rows per coach, plus team schedules for all those seasons. Rest days had to be derived rather than read — I left-joined each team’s schedule onto the player’s game log, so a game on the schedule with no minutes attached is a missed game. Rate limits meant sleeping between requests and caching every pull to CSV.

Method

A PyTorch LSTM — five input features, 16 hidden units, one layer, sequence length five — predicting whether a player misses his next game from minutes played, rest days, home/away, five-game rolling minutes, and field-goal attempts. The scaler was fit on training data only. Training used 63 player-seasons of Brown’s wings, around 5,000 games, holding out Harrison Barnes, Kevin Huerter and Trey Lyles from 2023–24 entirely. Raw sigmoid scores were Platt-scaled through a logistic regression so the outputs read as calibrated probabilities rather than arbitrary confidence. Bridges’ own season is inference only — he never missed a game, so he offers the model no positive examples to learn from.

What I found
  1. Only 18 of 582 players — about 3% — played all 82 games in 2025–26. Bridges sits 8th all-time at 638 consecutive games.
  2. His modelled risk never came close to the 15% decision threshold: 2.93% entering game 16, drifting to 4.41% entering game 82.
  3. On the three held-out players the model classified 287 of 304 games correctly at that threshold, catching 30 of 36 real absences against 11 false alarms.
  4. The coaching change was a non-event for durability. Under both Brown and Thibodeau, 82% of starters played 55+ games and 68% played 65+.
What I’d do differently

Three things. The reported numbers come from a plain binary cross-entropy loss on a heavily imbalanced target — I wrote a class-weighted version and never actually trained with it. The holdout is three players from a single team-season, which is enough to show the risk curve tracks real absences but far too small to quote a stable AUC, so I don’t quote one. And the model sees workload only: no injury history, no travel, no team rest policy — meaning it cannot tell “rested” apart from “hurt,” which is the distinction a team would actually pay for.

Article 03 · January 9, 2026 24-season trend analysis

G-League Call-Ups: A Way to Find Hidden Gems?

Read on the SAGB site →
The question

Two questions, really: does the G-League actually supply NBA value, and are teams calling up the right players when they go looking?

Data

Every G-League call-up from 2001–02 through 2024–25, scraped from gleague.nba.com across two different page formats and stitched into one table. Alongside it, Basketball-Reference advanced statistics for 2002–2025 — 14,885 player-seasons, scraped season by season — plus RotoWire injury counts by team, G-League per-player statistics, and both conferences’ standings. Joining call-ups to advanced stats meant reconciling roughly 286 name mismatches by hand.

Method

Call-up frequency by month and by season, cumulative and per-season trends, team-level call-up counts set against injury load, then overlaid distributions comparing each player’s G-League production to what they did in the NBA. Win-share distributions and boxplots measured what the call-ups were actually worth.

What I found
  1. Call-ups cluster in March (158) and December (148) historically, and barely happen in October (6).
  2. 2021–22 spiked to 164 call-ups under COVID hardship exceptions — 95 in December 2021 alone.
  3. 2024–25 saw 79 call-ups across 67 players. The five busiest teams — led by the 76ers at 10 — were all carrying heavy injury loads.
  4. The value is thin. Most call-ups land between 0.0 and 0.25 win shares for the season.
  5. Teams do select on production: called-up players averaged 31.4 G-League minutes per game against 27.2 for those never called, with a hard floor around 17.0. But only 36% carried even half their G-League minutes into the NBA.
What I’d do differently

The win-share comparisons pool players across 24 seasons with no era adjustment, and the 2021–22 hardship spike is a structural break that my trend lines treat as ordinary variation — it should be modelled as its own regime or excluded. The 286 hand-fixed name mismatches also mean the join isn’t reproducible from the raw files without my edits.

Article 02 · May 22, 2025 on/off lineup analysis

Russell Westbrook and His Resurgence with the Denver Nuggets

Read on the SAGB site →
The question

Westbrook arrived in Denver written off as finished. Was his 2024–25 an actual revival, or just what happens when you stand next to Nikola Jokić?

Data

Assembled by hand from five sources rather than scraped: with-or-without-you lineup combinations from PBP Stats, shooting percentiles from Cleaning the Glass, LEBRON impact ratings from BBall-Index, team ratings and league averages from Basketball-Reference, and efficiency metrics from nbarapm. Reconciling Excel’s date mangling and Basketball-Reference’s sort-tooltip column names took most of the cleaning effort.

Method

Deliberately descriptive — there is no model here. Lineup-level offensive and net ratings were benchmarked against the computed league average, career trends were plotted against league-average reference lines, and Westbrook was placed in an age cohort of 35-to-37-year-old shot creators on offensive versus defensive impact.

What I found
  1. Westbrook alongside Jokić and Jamal Murray produced a 128.68 offensive rating — about +14 on league average — and a net rating of +5.68.
  2. He posted a career-best 51.0% effective field goal percentage and 64.7% at the rim.
  3. The cost was real: a career-worst 18.9% turnover rate, which put him in the 2nd percentile among point guards for creation-adjusted turnovers.
  4. Denver’s rank in rim shot-making moved from 20th to 6th, and high-value assists from 27th to 16th, year over year.
What I’d do differently

These on/off splits are unadjusted for opponent strength or garbage time, and Westbrook’s minutes with Jokić were never randomly assigned — so what I measured are associations, not effects. With one season of lineup data the honest version of this article puts error bars on every rating, or drops the causal language entirely.

Article 01 · December 10, 2024 k-means clustering

Should Guards be Posting Up More Often?

Read on the SAGB site →
The question

Modern offenses have written the post-up off as a relic. For guards specifically — who almost never post up — is that verdict actually supported by their own efficiency numbers?

Data

Four seasons of Synergy Sports play-type tracking (2020–21 through 2023–24) scraped from nba.com/stats with Selenium, since the tables are JavaScript-rendered and invisible to a plain HTTP request. Merged against player bios, then filtered to a qualifying threshold of 10 minutes and 10 post-up possessions per game — 266 player-seasons after cleaning. Shooting splits by court zone and by five-foot range came from two further scrapes.

Method

k-means clustering with k = 3, chosen from an elbow curve over k = 1–10, run on points per possession against height and again on score frequency against height. The clusters were then used to define an efficient-guard cohort, whose post-up shooting was compared against their own three-point shooting across every zone of the floor.

What I found
  1. Guards average 0.96 points per possession on post-ups — they score on roughly half of them.
  2. The efficient cohort skews shorter and heavier, not taller: 9 of the top 12 post-up guards weigh over 200 lbs.
  3. Kyrie Irving leads at 1.43 PPP, with Bradley Beal and RJ Barrett both at 1.20.
  4. For most guards, post-up FG% clears 42% while their three-point percentage sits under 39% — and yet post-ups stay in the low single digits as a share of their offense.
What I’d do differently

I pulled three-point efficiency from the 25–29 ft column alone and left out the 20–24 ft band, which overstates the gap between the post-up and the three. The player-to-team join was also on name only, so multi-team seasons fanned out and I capped the result by slicing rather than keying on player-season.
